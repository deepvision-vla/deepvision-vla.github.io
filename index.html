Overview

A VLA can understand an instruction yet miss the object when it acts. Our analysis asks where task-relevant visual information becomes underused inside the model.

DeepVision-VLA reconnects deep action layers with a high-resolution vision expert. Shallow action attention selects useful visual regions before this deeper fusion.

Where does visual grounding weaken?

Looking inside OpenVLA, π₀ and QwenVLA-OFT.

Diagnosis

Shallow layers use visual evidence.

Masking task-region tokens in shallow layers causes a larger increase in action error.

Deep layers become less grounded.

Visual contribution becomes less task-focused, while masking has a smaller effect.

The design response: reinforce useful visual evidence where action prediction becomes less sensitive to it.

Reinforce vision. Preserve action detail.

VL-MoT + Action-Guided Visual Pruning

Method
↳

Multi-level visual injection

Couple the last 16 DINOv3 layers with the deepest 16 VLA layers through shared attention.

⌁

Action-guided selection

Aggregate shallow action-to-vision attention and retain the most relevant 50% of expert tokens.

⇉

Parallel action decoding

Preserve the expert and VLA pathways, then map action-token representations to continuous actions in parallel.

From simulation to real robots.

Stronger visual grounding supports precise and multi-stage manipulation.

Visual detail matters for both contact precision and the later steps of a task.

Success rates reported in the paper, Tables 1–2. Real-world mean averages the eight reported atomic task/step entries.

What makes the design work?Explore the ablations +

Deep visual fusion and late DINOv3 features work best in the controlled study. Action-conditioned pruning preserves useful regions, while averaging shallow attention layers gives a stronger cue than a single layer.

Controlled ablations use a separate protocol from the main results; see the paper for the full comparison.
